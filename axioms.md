# Thalean Mechanics Axioms

Version: 0.1.0

These axioms define the foundational assumptions of Thalean Mechanics.

They define what exists.

They do not define process.

---

## Axiom 1
### Primitive Computational Object

The primitive computational object is the thalion.

No computational object within Thalean Mechanics is more fundamental than the thalion.

---

## Axiom 2
### Canonical Address Space

Every thalion possesses a canonical address space consisting of exactly sixty canonical addresses organized as a 5 x 3 x 4 hierarchy.

The canonical address space is intrinsic to the thalion.

---

## Axiom 3
### Canonical Registration

Every thalion possesses exactly one canonical registration.

Canonical registration is objective.

---

## Axiom 4
### Observation

Observation is registration.

Observation does not create an alternative reality.

Observation produces registration.

---

## Axiom 5
### Representation

Representations are derived from canonical registration through registration interpreters.

Representations are not the thalion.

Representations do not modify canonical registration.

---

## Axiom 6
### Binary Representation

A binary value is a representation of canonical registration.

A bit is not a primitive computational object.

---

## Axiom 7
### Computational Records

Computational records preserve canonical registrations.

Computational records are not thalions.

Computational records do not constitute canonical registration.

---

## Axiom 8
### Context

A thalion may participate in multiple contexts.

Context influences interpretation.

Context does not redefine the thalion.

---

## Axiom 9
### Ontological Ordering

Thalion

↓

Canonical Registration

↓

Computational Record

↓

Representation

---

## Axiom 10
### Minimality

The ontology of the thalion is minimal.

Further ablation invalidates the thalion.

Further definition belongs to mechanics rather than ontology.

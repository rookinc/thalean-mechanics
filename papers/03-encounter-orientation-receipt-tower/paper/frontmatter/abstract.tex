\begin{abstract}
We join two finite constructions that each contain four visible states
but carry different algebra. The encounter quotient is the Klein four
group \(E\cong C_2\times C_2\); it records two participating roles but
forgets their order. The orientation quotient is the cyclic group
\(O\cong C_4\); it records a directed four-phase circuit. These groups
are not isomorphic and must not be identified.

Let a connected deterministic binary lift of \(O\) have nontrivial
circuit holonomy, and let its successor be \(H\). The lift is uniquely
\(C_8\), up to fiber-preserving gauge and isomorphism. If
\(H=AB\) for involutions \(A\) and \(B\), then
\(\langle A,B\rangle\cong D_{16}\), \(H^{-1}=BA\), and
\[
c=[A,B]=H^2,
\qquad
\tau=c^2=H^4.
\]
The element \(c\) is invisible in the encounter quotient and appears
as the orientation half-turn. Its square \(\tau\) is invisible in both
visible quotients but is the unique nontrivial binary deck
transformation upstairs. Thus the commutator residue of the ordered
encounter and the binary return receipt of the orientation lift are
successive levels of one exact finite tower. No physical
identification is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Voltage graphs separate two kinds of information.  A base graph records
which local moves are visible, while a receipt attached to an edge
records how a lifted state changes inside its fiber.  Circuit products
then measure what a closed visible walk carries home.  For a connected
base graph \(X\) and a finite receipt group \(R\), the standard theory
classifies regular \(R\)-lifts by conjugacy classes of representations
\[
  \pi_1(X)\longrightarrow R.
\]
The image of the representation controls connectedness and component
size \cite{gross-tucker,godsil-royle}.

That theorem begins after the apparatus has been specified.  It does
not tell us which receipt group, quotient, projection, or voltage should
be assigned to an unlabeled graph.  This paper isolates that missing
binding step.  We place the previously established finite receipt
algebra beside a native graph \(\Gsixty\), enumerate every admissible
semiregular action before consulting the historical quotient labels,
and allow the graph to return a spectrum rather than forcing a desired
answer.

The blind spectrum is many, not one.  This is an important result
rather than an inconvenience.  It prevents the later recovery of
\(\Gthirty\) and \(\Gfifteen\) from being stated as an unconditional
property of the raw graph.  Selection becomes unique only after two
premises are declared: the receipt rank and covariance under the full
native automorphism group.

Within the binary subspectrum, symmetry leaves one normal \(\Ctwo\)
action.  Within the four-state subspectrum, symmetry leaves one normal
\(\Vfour\) action.  These actions were registered before their
historical quotient identities were compared.  Unblinding then shows
that they are precisely the old deck structures, recovered under a new
and independently imposed algebraic language.

The resulting statement is an exact tower theorem.  Both quotient
layers are recovered, both native voltage systems reconstruct
\(\Gsixty\), both holonomy images are surjective, and the subgroup
inclusion factors the quotient maps.  The proof is finite and
certificate-backed.  Its limits are equally explicit: it does not
derive the words binary or \(\Vfour\) from the raw graph, select an
orientation, or attach a physical meaning to the receipts.

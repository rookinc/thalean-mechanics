\section{Boundary and conclusion}

We have classified deterministic binary lifts of every finite directed cycle.
The local voltage assignments contain apparent freedom, but gauge removes all
of it except one binary circuit sum. That sum separates two and only two
possibilities: disconnected duplication or connected doubling.

The theorem does not explain why a particular system supplies a visible
cycle, why its receipt fiber is binary, or why its circuit holonomy is
nontrivial. Those are independent structural premises. Once they are given,
however, the lifted order is no longer discretionary.

No interpretation in terms of physical action, time, space, energy, matter,
gravity, or radiation is part of the theorem. Any such representation must
first construct the visible cycle, binary deck action, deterministic lift, and
nontrivial receipt without assuming the doubled cycle it seeks to derive.

The earned statement is concise:
\begin{quote}
A visible cycle closes after (n) steps. A nontrivial binary receipt closes
only after (2n).
\end{quote}

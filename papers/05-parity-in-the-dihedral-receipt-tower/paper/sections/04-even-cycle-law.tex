\section{The even-cycle law}

Suppose (n) is even. Then (n/2) is an integer and
\[
\comm^{n/2}
=(H^2)^{n/2}
=H^n
=\receipt.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Even receipt inclusion]
If (n) is even, the binary return receipt lies in the commutator subgroup:
\[
\receipt\in\langle\comm\rangle,
\qquad
\receipt=\comm^{n/2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

Thus the successive powers
\[
H,qquad
\comm=H^2,
\qquad
\receipt=H^n
\]
form a nested receipt tower. The order-sensitive commutator can be iterated
until it reaches the hidden binary return.

This does not mean that the commutator and binary receipt are identical in
general. Their orders are (n) and (2), respectively. It means that the
binary receipt is the distinguished order-two element inside the cyclic
commutator subgroup whenever the visible cycle length is even.

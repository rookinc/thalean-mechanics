\section{Involutive dihedral completion}

Fix (n\geq3). Let (A) and (B) satisfy
\[
A^2=B^2=1,
\qquad
H=AB,
\qquad
|H|=2n.
\]
The reverse ordered product is
\[
H^{-1}=(AB)^{-1}=BA.
\]
Moreover,
\[
AHA=A(AB)A=BA=H^{-1}.
\]
Thus conjugation by (A) reverses the lifted cyclic evolution.

\begin{proposition}[Dihedral completion]
The generated group has the normal forms
\[
H^k
\quad\text{and}\quad
AH^k,
\qquad 0\leq k<2n,
\]
and is the dihedral group of order (4n):
\[
\langle A,B\rangle
\cong
\langle H,A\mid H^{2n}=A^2=1,\ AHA=H^{-1}\rangle.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since (B=AH), the group is generated by (A) and (H). The relation
(AH=H^{-1}A) reduces every word to one of the displayed forms. The powers
of (H) are distinct because (H) has order (2n). If
(AH^i=H^j), then (A\in\langle H\rangle). It would then commute with (H),
contradicting (AHA=H^{-1}\neq H). Hence the two normal-form families are
disjoint, giving exactly (4n) elements and the stated dihedral group.
\end{proof}

We use ``dihedral group of order (4n)'' explicitly to avoid competing
subscript conventions.

\section{Boundary and conclusion}

An order-(2n) evolver factored by two involutions has a forced dihedral
completion. Its commutator receipt and binary return receipt are always
well-defined, but their relationship is not uniform across cycle lengths.
Even cycles place the binary receipt inside the commutator tower. Odd cycles
split the two receipts into independent factors that together reconstruct the
evolver.

This parity distinction also governs visible encounter information. The
natural Klein-four quotient hides the binary return exactly in the even case.
Thus the four-phase construction is not merely one example of cycle doubling;
it belongs to the even branch of a general dihedral receipt theorem.

The theorem does not derive its involutions, lifted order, or cycle length from
a physical system. It assigns no meaning involving space, time, energy,
particles, gravity, emission, or force. A proposed representation must bind
those premises independently before importing the parity conclusions.

The earned statement is:
\begin{quote}
The commutator reaches the hidden receipt exactly when the visible cycle is
even.
\end{quote}
